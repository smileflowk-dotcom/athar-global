# ATHAR V4 — first three IRA900 formulations: pre-laboratory scientific decision (2026-10-12)

**Scope:** No new GPU, ML fitting, atomistic run or sample order. This is a cautious reading of existing experimental publications and our V4 evidence. The test dossiers and original A–E computational gates remain unchanged.

## Why these three are references, not necessarily discoveries

Hegarty et al., **Environmental Science & Technology 2023**, DOI https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.3c02543, *Expanding the Library of Ions for Moisture-Swing Carbon Capture*:
- Dibasic phosphate was the strongest by **mass-normalized** moisture-swing CO2 capacity in that study;
- Tribasic phosphate performed best when **kinetics** were considered;
- Pyrophosphate had the highest performance **per ion or per unit charge**.
These are distinct performance objectives. Do not infer a universal winner from any of these or claim new discovery of their ion families. Exact polymer grade, test conditions and counterion protonation must match before direct reproduction comparisons.

Hegarty et al., **Environmental Science & Technology 2026**, DOI https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.5c16820:
- Ten commercial resins were compared under humidity swings;
- Type-I functional groups tended to show higher swing capacities with phosphate; Type II preferred carbonate **within that study**;
- Backbone, porosity and kinetics change conclusions; IRA910 Type II should not inherit the IRA900 Type I site model or scores.

IRA900 carbonate experimental baseline: **ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering 2025** DOI https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.5c00227:
- Demonstrated 1.92 mmol CO2/g at 20% RH and 25 C for *their* IRA-900-C sample, not a measured result of ATHAR formulations;
- Describes potential resin decomposition/trimethylamine odour when immersing IRA900 in strongly basic 0.5 M K2CO3, followed by use of KHCO3 instead. This is a published caution, NOT a validated production recipe for the three phosphates.

**Scientific interpretation:** These are well-justified **benchmark experiment requests**. They have not all cleared the same atomistic robustness gates, and they are not three new molecular discoveries.

## One reproducible laboratory request (PI must adapt and approve)

1. **Identity of source commercial resin:** supplier, as-supplied ion form, exact Type I or II site, matrix, particle size, macroporosity vs gel, lot/COA/SDS.
2. **Three separately made batches per form**, plus resin-matched carbonate/bicarbonate form and blank resin/process controls; safety review and a justified preparation route with pH and competitive-ion mass balance. Ion exchange cannot be verified merely from reagent stoichiometry.
3. **Measure actual ion loading and speciation:** ICP-OES phosphorus plus species-discriminating assay when possible; pH and leached ions; pre/post-cycling stability. For P2O7, distinguish retained pyro from phosphate.
4. **CO2-specific measured uptake/release:** constant CO2 partial pressure and temperature, calibrated dynamic breakthrough or appropriate isotherm; initial proposed 20% RH vs 65% RH with laboratory-defined setpoints and equilibration, report dry-sorbent and active-site-normalized work capacity with uncertainty. Independent batches and repeated cycles; include regeneration energy/steps if measurable.
5. **Pre-register objective-specific rankings:** (a) mmol CO2 / g dry resin per RH swing, (b) net uptake/release speed and cyclic productivity, (c) mmol CO2 / equivalent exchange site, (d) stability, safety and estimated material/handling cost. Do NOT combine incomparable literature values or cherry-pick a single winner.
6. **GO** to expanded panel only for reproducible blank-corrected CO2-specific effects with valid ion loading and uncertainty; **HOLD** for indeterminate concentration/geometry or insufficient replicates; **NO-GO** for verified hazards, unstable chemical identity or non-working adsorption. Only laboratory measurement can establish any of these.
